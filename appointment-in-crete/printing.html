Printing the book: printers, costs and how to order

A plain guide for Clare: who can print and bind the book in the UK, what it costs, how to ask for a quote, and what to check before ordering. Everything here was looked up in October 2026; the printers’ own quotes are what count, and I am glad to handle the quoting and file preparation.

What I would do

For twenty-odd hardbacks that look and feel like the 1953 book, ask three printers for quotes on the same specification (below) and compare: 4edge in Essex (the only printer whose published menu matches the 1953 book item for item, from ten copies), Imprint Digital in Exeter (publishes its prices: about £15 a copy at 25 for cloth and jacket, minimum 25) and TJ Books in Padstow (trade quality, quote only). All three do real cloth, gold foil on the spine, printed endpapers, sewn binding and a printed dust jacket on cream paper, and all take a PDF from a private customer. Expect roughly £15–25 a copy at 20–25 copies. Keep Bookvault in reserve: thinner paper and mixed reviews, but real cloth and foil one copy at a time for years to come, which suits “more grandchildren along the way”.

If the budget matters more than cloth: a hardback with a printed, laminated case and a real dust jacket from Mixam (no account needed, instant online price) or from IngramSpark (cheapest, but someone has to hold a publisher account and buy an ISBN) comes to about £8–12 a copy.

Paperbacks with the jacket design printed as the cover cost about £4–5 a copy from Mixam or IngramSpark, and are a sensible way to give copies to everyone while the hardbacks go to the grandchildren.

For one or two presentation copies, a hand bindery (Blissetts, Wyvern) will case-bind printed sheets in cloth by hand for £40–60 a copy on top of printing.

Whichever route: order a single proof copy first, check it against the original, then order the run. I will prepare the files to the chosen printer’s templates (they each want the jacket and cover laid out to their own spine-width figure) and can deal with the printer directly if you would like.

The specification to send

Printers quote quickly and accurately when they are given the specification in their own language. This is the text to send, by their quote form or by email; I can send it for you.

Subject: Quote request — short-run hardback with dust jacket, 240 pp

I would like a quote for a short run of a facsimile reprint of a 1953 memoir, for private family use (not for sale). Print-ready PDFs will be supplied (text, jacket, endpapers).

Quantities: 10, 20 and 50 copies, please price each.
Trim size: 130 × 207 mm (portrait). If you cannot do a custom size, please quote A5 (148 × 210 mm) as well.
Extent: 240 pages plus 2 pages of prelims if needed; black only throughout.
Text paper: 80 or 90 gsm cream book wove (e.g. Bookwove Cream, Munken Pure, Enso Cream).
Binding: case-bound hardback, sewn if possible (otherwise PUR), square back, head and tail bands.
Case: cloth (Wibalin Buckram or similar) — please give colour options; gold foil blocking on the spine (two lines of text).
Endpapers: printed, one colour (black) on cream, front and back (the same map image on both).
Dust jacket: full colour (CMYK) on 150–170 gsm uncoated or silk, matt laminated or unlaminated — please quote both; flaps 75–80 mm.
Proof: one printed and bound proof copy before the run, please price separately.
Delivery: to one UK address.

Please also tell me: your spine-width figure for 240 pp on the paper quoted (for the jacket file), your jacket and cover templates, your turnaround, and whether you have a sample of a cloth hardback with a jacket I could see.

Many thanks,

Why these choices: cream paper and a sewn cloth case are what the 1953 book had; a laminated jacket resists finger marks, an unlaminated one looks more like the original. Printed endpapers front and back cost little and avoid a plain back endpaper.

Print-on-demand platforms

Online services that print one copy at a time at a fixed price. Cheapest, and easy to reorder, but with less choice of materials and size. An ISBN is not needed for private copies at any of the printers above (IngramSpark lets you mark the title “non-distributable”). If one is ever wanted, Nielsen sells a single UK ISBN for £93 or a block of ten for £174 (Nielsen ISBN Store).

Before you say yes to a printer

  1. Ask for a sample. Most short-run printers post a free sample pack or a sample book. Feel the paper and the cloth before you decide.
  2. Insist on a proof copy (one finished book) before the run; check the page size against the original, the position of the type on the page, the darkness of the type, the jacket fit and the spine lettering.
  3. Cream, not white, paper for the text; 80–90 gsm. White paper makes a reprint look like an office print.
  4. Sewn rather than glued if the price difference is small: sewn books open flat and last for generations; glued (“PUR”) books are fine but stiffer.
  5. Jacket paper: 150–170 gsm. Thinner tears; thicker will not fold cleanly.
  6. Who owns what. Confirm the price includes delivery and VAT (books are zero-rated for VAT in the UK; jackets and endpapers printed with them normally are too).
  7. Timing. Short-run printers typically take 3–4 weeks from approved proof; print-on-demand a week or two. Christmas slots fill in October and November.
  8. Reorders. Ask whether the files are kept so that further copies can be ordered later at the same price, and what the minimum is for a reorder.

The words printers use

  • Case-bound / casebound: a hardback; the pages (“book block”) are glued or sewn into a stiff case.
  • Cloth / Wibalin / Buckram: book-cloth coverings. Wibalin is a widely used paper-based cloth-look material; Buckram is a heavier, more textured woven cloth. Both take gold foil well.
  • Printed case / PPC / casewrap / case laminate: a hardback whose cover is a printed, laminated paper wrapped round the boards instead of cloth. Cheaper; no jacket needed, though one can be added.
  • Foil blocking: lettering stamped into the cloth with gold (or other) foil, as on the original spine.
  • Dust jacket / dust wrapper: the printed paper wrapper with flaps.
  • Endpapers: the double leaves pasted inside the boards; in this book the front one carries the maps.
  • Sewn / Smyth-sewn: the folded sections are stitched with thread before casing in. PUR / perfect binding: pages glued at the spine with a strong polyurethane adhesive.
  • Trim size: the finished page size. Extent: number of pages. gsm: paper weight in grams per square metre.
  • Bleed: printing that runs off the edge of the page (the jacket has it; the text pages do not). Spine width: depends on page count and paper; the printer gives the figure and the jacket is laid out to it.
  • Print on demand (POD): printing one copy at a time to order, at a fixed unit price, instead of a run.
  • ISBN: the book-trade number; only needed if a book is to be sold or if a particular platform insists on it. Not needed for private copies.
